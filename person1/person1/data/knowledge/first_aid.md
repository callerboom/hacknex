# Offline Emergency First-Aid Guidelines & Procedures
> **MANDATORY MEDICAL DISCLAIMER**: This offline reference is for educational and emergency guidance only and is **not professional medical advice**. In any life-threatening situation or severe emergency, immediately call your local emergency services (e.g., 911, 112, 999, 108) before attempting non-certified interventions.

---

## 1. Emergency Action Plan: DRSABCD
When approaching an emergency scene:
1. **D - Danger**: Ensure the area is safe for yourself, bystanders, and the casualty.
2. **R - Response**: Check responsiveness by gently shaking shoulders and asking loudly, *"Are you okay?"*
3. **S - Send for Help**: Call emergency services immediately or direct a bystander to call.
4. **A - Airway**: Check and open airway by tilting the head back slightly and lifting the chin (if no spinal injury is suspected).
5. **B - Breathing**: Look, listen, and feel for normal breathing for up to 10 seconds.
6. **C - CPR**: If unconscious and not breathing normally, start chest compressions immediately.
7. **D - Defibrillation**: Apply an Automated External Defibrillator (AED) as soon as available; follow voice prompts.

---

## 2. Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)
### Adult CPR
- **Rate**: 100 to 120 compressions per minute (to the beat of *"Stayin' Alive"*).
- **Depth**: At least 2 inches (5 cm) but no more than 2.4 inches (6 cm).
- **Ratio**: 30 chest compressions followed by 2 rescue breaths (or continuous Hands-Only CPR if untrained).
- **Position**: Place heel of one hand in center of chest (lower half of sternum), interlock fingers with second hand, keep elbows straight, position shoulders directly over hands.
- **Chest Recoil**: Allow complete chest recoil after each compression without bouncing.

### Child & Infant CPR
- **Child (1 to puberty)**: Use 1 or 2 hands to compress chest approx. 2 inches deep.
- **Infant (< 1 year)**: Use 2 fingers in center of chest; compress approx. 1.5 inches deep.

## 3. Choking Management
### Conscious Adult or Child (> 1 year)
1. **Encourage Coughing**: If casualty is coughing forcefully, encourage them to keep coughing.
2. **Back Blows**:
   - Lean the person forward.
   - Deliver **5 firm back blows** between shoulder blades using the heel of your hand.
3. **Abdominal Thrusts (Heimlich Maneuver)**:
   - Stand behind the person, wrap arms around waist.
   - Make a fist with one hand, place thumb side just above navel (below ribcage).
   - Grasp fist with other hand and press hard inward and upward in **5 quick thrusts**.
4. **Alternate**: Repeat 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until obstruction clears or person becomes unconscious.

### Infant Choking (< 1 year)
- Place infant face down along forearm resting on your thigh, supporting head and jaw.
- Deliver **5 gentle back blows** between shoulder blades.
- Turn infant face up, give **5 chest thrusts** using 2 fingers in center of chest.

## 4. Burns & Scalds Treatment
### Immediate Action (Cool the Burn)
- **Cool Running Water**: Hold burn under cool (not ice-cold) running tap water for **at least 20 minutes**.
- **Remove Constrictions**: Remove rings, watches, tight clothing before swelling begins (do NOT remove clothing stuck to burned skin).
- **Cover**: Cover loosely with sterile, non-adherent dressing or clean plastic cling wrap.
- **Keep Warm**: Keep casualty warm with a blanket over unburned areas to prevent hypothermia.

### Critical Burn Rules (What NOT to Do)
- **DO NOT** apply ice, iced water, butter, oil, toothpaste, or ointments to fresh burns.
- **DO NOT** pop or prick blisters.
- **Seek Emergency Care For**: Burns on face, hands, feet, groin, major joints; electrical or chemical burns; or any deep/charred burn.

---

## 5. Severe Bleeding & Hemorrhage Control
1. **Direct Pressure**: Apply firm, continuous direct pressure onto the wound using a sterile gauze pad, clean cloth, or bare hands if nothing else is available.
2. **Maintain Pressure**: Do not remove soaked dressings; add more pads on top and press firmly.
3. **Secure Dressing**: Wrap firmly with a bandage to maintain pressure while checking distal circulation.
4. **Tourniquet (Life-Threatening Limb Bleeding)**:
   - Apply commercial tourniquet 2–3 inches above wound (never directly over a joint).
   - Tighten until bright red spurting bleeding stops and record the exact time of application.

---

## 6. Snake Bites & Venous Envenomation
- **DO**:
  - Keep the victim calm and completely still to slow venom spread through the lymphatic system.
  - Immobilize the bitten limb with a splint and apply a broad pressure bandage firmly from fingers/toes moving upward (Pressure Immobilization Technique - PIT).
  - Keep bitten area at or below heart level.
  - Transport to hospital immediately; note snake description if safe without catching it.
- **DO NOT**:
  - **DO NOT** cut the bite wound.
  - **DO NOT** attempt to suck out venom.
  - **DO NOT** apply ice or a narrow tourniquet.
  - **DO NOT** wash the bite area (residual venom helps clinicians identify correct antivenom).

---

## 7. Fractures, Sprains & Strains (R.I.C.E.)
- **R - Rest**: Stop physical activity and prevent weight bearing.
- **I - Ice**: Apply a cold pack wrapped in a cloth for 15–20 minutes every 2–3 hours.
- **C - Compression**: Wrap an elastic compression bandage snugly (not too tight).
- **E - Elevation**: Elevate injured limb above heart level when resting to reduce swelling.
- **Fracture Suspected**: If bone is deformed, protruding (open fracture), or causes severe pain on touch, immobilize in position found with splint/padding and seek emergency care.

## 8. Heat Exhaustion vs. Heat Stroke
| Symptom | Heat Exhaustion | Heat Stroke (CRITICAL EMERGENCY) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Skin** | Cool, pale, heavy sweating | Hot, red, dry skin (or damp from exertion) |
| **Pulse** | Rapid, weak | Rapid, strong/bounding |
| **Mental State** | Dizziness, fatigue, headache | Confusion, altered behavior, seizures, unconsciousness |
| **Body Temp** | Usually < 40°C (104°F) | **High > 40°C (104°F)** |
| **Action** | Move to cool area, loosen clothes, sip cool water. | **Call emergency services immediately!** Rapid immersion in cold water or apply ice packs to neck, armpits, and groin. |

---

## 9. Seizures & Convulsions
- **During Seizure**:
  - Protect the person from injury: clear hard or sharp objects away.
  - Cushion head with folded jacket or soft item.
  - Loosen tight neckwear.
  - Time the duration of the seizure.
- **DO NOT**:
  - **DO NOT** restrain the person or hold them down.
  - **DO NOT** put anything in their mouth (they cannot swallow their tongue).
- **After Seizure**:
  - Once convulsions stop, place person into the **Recovery Position** (on side) to maintain an open airway.
  - Call emergency services if seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes, if another seizure follows immediately, or if the person is injured or pregnant.

---

## 10. Anaphylaxis (Severe Allergic Reaction)
- **Signs**: Swelling of face/lips/throat, difficulty breathing, wheezing, hives, dizziness, rapid drop in blood pressure.
- **Steps**:
  1. Have person sit or lie down flat with legs elevated (unless breathing is easier sitting upright).
  2. If an Epinephrine Auto-Injector (EpiPen) is available:
     - Pull off blue safety cap.
     - Place orange tip against outer mid-thigh at a 90-degree angle.
     - Push firmly until click is heard; hold firmly for 3–5 seconds.
     - Massage injection site for 10 seconds.
  3. Call emergency services immediately even if symptoms appear to improve.
