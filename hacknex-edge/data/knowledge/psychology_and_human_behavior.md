## 1. Major Psychological Frameworks and Foundational Theories

- **Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs**:
  - Proposed by Abraham Maslow (1943); human motivation progresses through hierarchical levels:
    1. **Physiological Needs**: Air, water, food, shelter, sleep, clothing, homeostasis.
    2. **Safety Needs**: Personal security, financial resources, employment, health, property.
    3. **Love and Belonging**: Friendship, intimacy, family, social connection, sense of belonging.
    4. **Esteem Needs**: Self-respect, status, recognition, strength, freedom, accomplishment.
    5. **Self-Actualization**: Fulfilling personal potential, creativity, moral growth, peak experiences.

- **Cognitive Psychology & Dual-Process Theory**:
  - Developed by Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky (*Thinking, Fast and Slow*):
    - **System 1 (Fast Thinking)**: Automatic, intuitive, unconscious, effortless, emotion-driven (e.g., detecting hostility in a voice, reading billboard words).
    - **System 2 (Slow Thinking)**: Deliberate, analytical, conscious, effortful, logical calculation (e.g., solving $17 \times 24$, comparing complex investments).

- **Behavioral Psychology (Conditioning)**:
  - **Classical Conditioning (Ivan Pavlov)**: Learning through association; an unconditioned stimulus (food) paired with conditioned stimulus (bell) creates a conditioned response (salivation).
  - **Operant Conditioning (B.F. Skinner)**: Learning through consequences; positive reinforcement (rewarding desirable behavior), negative reinforcement (removing adverse stimuli), positive/negative punishment.

- **Psychodynamic Theory (Sigmund Freud)**:
  - Structure of the psyche: **Id** (pleasure principle, primal instincts), **Ego** (reality principle, mediating reality), and **Superego** (moral conscience, societal standards).
  - Defense mechanisms: Repression, rationalization, projection, sublimation, displacement.

## 2. Cognitive Biases, Heuristics, and Mental Models

- **Confirmation Bias**: The tendency to search for, interpret, favor, and recall information that confirms preexisting beliefs while ignoring contrary evidence.
- **Anchoring Effect**: Disproportionate reliance on the first piece of information encountered (the "anchor") when making subsequent judgments or financial negotiations.
- **Availability Heuristic**: Overestimating the likelihood of events based on how easily recent or memorable examples come to mind (e.g., fearing plane crashes after news reports).
- **Dunning-Kruger Effect**: A cognitive bias where people with low ability at a task overestimate their competence, while experts tend to underestimate their relative superiority.
- **Loss Aversion**: Psychologically, the pain of losing is twice as impactful as the pleasure of gaining an equivalent amount (e.g., losing $100 feels worse than winning $100 feels good).
- **Fundamental Attribution Error**: Overemphasizing personal characteristics and underestimating situational factors when judging others' actions.

## 3. Emotional Intelligence (EQ) & Interpersonal Dynamics

- **Daniel Goleman's Five Components of Emotional Intelligence**:
  1. **Self-Awareness**: Recognizing one's own emotions, strengths, weaknesses, and their impact on decisions.
  2. **Self-Regulation**: Managing disruptive impulses, adapting to change, and maintaining composure under stress.
  3. **Motivation**: Internal drive to achieve for personal growth rather than external rewards.
  4. **Empathy**: Understanding the emotional makeup of other people and treating them accordingly.
  5. **Social Skills**: Managing relationships, building rapport, active listening, and conflict resolution.

- **Active Listening Principles**:
  - Paraphrasing: Reflecting the speaker's thoughts back in your own words to confirm understanding.
  - Non-verbal validation: Maintaining appropriate eye contact, nodding, and open body posture.
  - Deferring judgment: Avoiding premature interruption, advice-giving, or defensiveness.

## 4. Stress Management, Coping Mechanisms & Mental Well-being

- **Physiological Stress Response (The Nervous System)**:
  - **Sympathetic Nervous System (Fight-or-Flight)**: Amygdala triggers hypothalamus to release adrenaline and cortisol; heart rate increases, pupils dilate, digestion slows.
  - **Parasympathetic Nervous System (Rest-and-Digest)**: Vagus nerve activation slows heart rate, lowers blood pressure, and restores physiological homeostasis.

- **Evidence-Based Grounding & Calming Techniques**:
  - **Box Breathing (Navy SEAL Technique)**: Inhale for 4 seconds -> Hold for 4 seconds -> Exhale for 4 seconds -> Hold empty for 4 seconds. Repeat for 4 cycles.
  - **Physiological Sigh (Andrew Huberman)**: Two quick inhales through the nose followed by a long, slow exhalation through the mouth. Rapidly balances carbon dioxide and heart rate.
  - **5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Grounding**: Name 5 things you can see, 4 things you can touch, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, and 1 thing you can taste.
  - **Cognitive Reframing (CBT Principle)**: Identifying automatic negative thoughts (catastrophizing, black-and-white thinking) and replacing them with objective, balanced evidence.

## 5. Developmental Stages & Human Growth

- **Erik Erikson's Stages of Psychosocial Development**:
  - Trust vs. Mistrust (Infancy): Developing hope through consistent caregiver support.
  - Autonomy vs. Shame/Doubt (Early Childhood): Developing will and independence.
  - Initiative vs. Guilt (Preschool): Purpose and exploration.
  - Industry vs. Inferiority (School Age): Competence and skill development.
  - Identity vs. Role Confusion (Adolescence): Developing self-concept and personal values.
  - Intimacy vs. Isolation (Young Adulthood): Building meaningful romantic and social bonds.
  - Generativity vs. Stagnation (Middle Adulthood): Contributing to future generations and society.
  - Integrity vs. Despair (Late Adulthood): Reflecting on life with wisdom and closure.
