## Burns

Cool water should be run over a minor burn for ten to twenty minutes immediately after the injury. Do not use ice, butter, toothpaste, or any home remedy on a burn. Cover the burn loosely with a sterile non-stick bandage or clean cloth. Take over-the-counter pain relief such as ibuprofen or paracetamol if needed. Seek medical help for burns larger than 3 inches, burns on face, hands, feet, genitals, or major joints, or any third-degree burn.

## Fever Management

A fever is a temperature above 38 degrees Celsius or 100.4 degrees Fahrenheit. Rest and drink plenty of fluids such as water, juice, or broth. Use paracetamol or ibuprofen to reduce fever in adults. Tepid sponging with lukewarm water can help lower body temperature. Seek medical attention if fever exceeds 39.5 C, lasts more than 3 days, or is accompanied by severe headache, stiff neck, or rash.

## Dehydration

Signs of dehydration include dark urine, dry mouth, dizziness, fatigue, and decreased urination. Mild dehydration can be treated by drinking water or oral rehydration solution slowly. Avoid alcohol and caffeine as they worsen dehydration. For severe dehydration with confusion, rapid heartbeat, or sunken eyes, seek emergency care immediately.

## Headache

Most tension headaches can be managed with rest, hydration, and over-the-counter pain relief. Apply a cold or warm compress to the forehead or neck. Reduce screen time and ensure good lighting. Migraine headaches may require prescribed medication. Seek urgent care for sudden severe headache, headache after head injury, or headache with confusion, fever, or vision changes.

## Cold and Flu

Rest and stay hydrated with warm fluids such as broth, herbal tea, and water. Use saline nasal spray or steam inhalation to relieve congestion. Honey and lemon in warm water can soothe a sore throat. Paracetamol or ibuprofen helps with body aches and fever. Antibiotics do not work against viral infections. See a doctor if symptoms worsen after 7 days or you develop difficulty breathing or chest pain.

## Sore Throat

Gargle with warm salt water several times a day. Drink warm fluids and suck on throat lozenges. Avoid smoking and secondhand smoke. Most sore throats caused by viruses resolve within a week. See a doctor if throat pain is severe, you have difficulty swallowing or breathing, or symptoms last longer than one week.

## Allergies

Common allergy symptoms include sneezing, runny nose, itchy eyes, and skin rash. Antihistamines can relieve mild to moderate allergy symptoms. Avoid known allergens and keep windows closed during high pollen days. For severe allergic reactions with swelling of face or throat or difficulty breathing, call emergency services immediately as this may be anaphylaxis.

## Nutrition and Hydration

Adults should drink 6 to 8 glasses of water per day. Eat a balanced diet with fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean protein. Reduce intake of processed foods, added sugar, and saturated fats. Vitamin D can be obtained from sunlight and fortified foods. Maintain regular meal times and avoid skipping breakfast.

## Sleep and Rest

Adults need 7 to 9 hours of sleep per night for optimal health. Maintain a consistent sleep schedule even on weekends. Avoid screens for at least one hour before bedtime. Keep the bedroom cool, dark, and quiet. Regular physical activity improves sleep quality.

## Stress Management

Regular physical exercise reduces stress hormones and improves mood. Practice deep breathing or meditation for at least 10 minutes daily. Maintain social connections with friends and family. Limit alcohol and avoid recreational drugs as coping mechanisms. Seek professional help if stress leads to persistent anxiety or depression.

## Eye Strain

The 20-20-20 rule helps reduce digital eye strain: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Blink regularly to keep eyes moist. Ensure good lighting when using screens. Position screens at arm's length and slightly below eye level. Consult an eye doctor if strain persists or vision changes occur.

## Back Pain

Maintain good posture while sitting and standing. Use an ergonomic chair with lumbar support. Take short breaks every 30 to 60 minutes when sitting for long periods. Gentle stretching and strengthening exercises help prevent back pain. Apply ice in the first 48 hours of acute injury, then switch to heat. Seek medical advice for back pain with numbness, tingling, or leg weakness.
