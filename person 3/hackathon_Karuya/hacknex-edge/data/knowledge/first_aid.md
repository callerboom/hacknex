## CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

Call emergency services immediately before starting CPR. Place the person on their back on a firm surface. Tilt the head back gently to open the airway and check for breathing. If not breathing normally, begin chest compressions by placing the heel of one hand on the center of the chest. Press down at least 5 centimeters at a rate of 100 to 120 compressions per minute. After 30 compressions give 2 rescue breaths if trained. Continue until emergency services arrive or the person recovers. An AED should be used as soon as one is available.

## Choking — Adult

Stand behind the person and give 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand. If the blockage is not cleared give 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver). Alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the object is dislodged. If the person becomes unconscious begin CPR. Do not perform abdominal thrusts on pregnant women or infants.

## Bleeding Control

Apply direct firm pressure to the wound using a clean cloth or bandage. Do not remove the cloth if it becomes soaked — add more on top. Elevate the injured limb above heart level if possible. Maintain pressure for at least 15 minutes without peeking. Seek emergency help for deep wounds, arterial bleeding (bright red spurting blood), or wounds that do not stop bleeding.

## Sprains and Strains

Follow the RICE method: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation. Rest the injured area and avoid putting weight on it. Apply ice wrapped in a cloth for 15 to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours. Use a compression bandage to reduce swelling. Elevate the limb above heart level when possible. Take ibuprofen for pain and swelling. Seek medical care if there is severe pain, inability to bear weight, or suspected fracture.

## Fractures

Immobilize the injured area — do not attempt to realign the bone. Use a splint or padding to prevent movement. Apply ice wrapped in cloth to reduce swelling. Do not give food or drink in case surgery is needed. Seek emergency care for open fractures where bone pierces the skin, fractures of the spine, pelvis, or femur, or any fracture in a child.

## Seizures

Clear the area of hard or sharp objects to prevent injury. Place the person on their side to keep the airway clear and prevent choking. Do not restrain the person or put anything in their mouth. Time the seizure. Stay calm and reassure bystanders. After the seizure ends, stay with the person until they are fully conscious. Call emergency services if the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, there is no recovery, or it is the person's first seizure.

## Unconsciousness

Check for danger before approaching. Tap the shoulder firmly and shout to assess response. Call emergency services immediately if unresponsive. Open the airway by tilting the head back and lifting the chin. Check for normal breathing for up to 10 seconds. If breathing, place in the recovery position on their side with the top knee bent forward. If not breathing, start CPR immediately.

## Anaphylaxis (Severe Allergic Reaction)

Symptoms include throat swelling, difficulty breathing, rapid heartbeat, and severe drop in blood pressure. Use an epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) immediately if available by injecting into the outer thigh. Call emergency services even after using the EpiPen. Lay the person flat with legs elevated unless they have breathing difficulty. A second EpiPen dose may be needed after 5 to 15 minutes if symptoms return. Do not let the person stand or walk.

## Burns — First Aid

Cool the burn under cool running water for at least 10 to 20 minutes immediately. Do not use ice, creams, or greasy substances on the burn. Remove jewellery or clothing near the burn unless stuck to the skin. Cover loosely with cling film or a clean non-fluffy material. Do not burst blisters. Seek emergency care for chemical or electrical burns, burns covering large areas of the body, or deep burns.

## Poisoning

Call Poison Control or emergency services immediately. Do not induce vomiting unless specifically instructed by medical personnel. Bring the container or substance label to the hospital. If the substance is on skin or eyes flush with large amounts of water for 15 to 20 minutes. If the person is unconscious and not breathing begin CPR.

## Heat Stroke

Heat stroke is a medical emergency. Move the person to a cool shaded area immediately. Call emergency services. Remove excess clothing. Cool the person rapidly using ice packs to neck, armpits, and groin or cool wet cloths. Fan the person continuously. Do not give fluids if the person is unconscious. Monitor breathing while waiting for help.

## Hypothermia

Move the person to a warm dry place immediately. Remove any wet clothing gently. Wrap in warm blankets including around the head but not covering the face. Offer warm sweet drinks if the person is conscious and able to swallow. Do not rub or massage limbs as this can cause cardiac arrest. Seek emergency medical care for severe shivering, confusion, slurred speech, or loss of consciousness.

## Eye Injury

For chemical splashes wash the eye immediately under running water for 15 to 20 minutes. Do not rub the injured eye. For a foreign body do not attempt to remove it with fingers. Cover loosely with a clean pad and seek medical attention. For a blunt eye injury apply a cold compress gently to reduce swelling. Seek emergency care immediately for penetrating eye injuries or any loss of vision.
