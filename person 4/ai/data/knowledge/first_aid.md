# First Aid Emergency Guide
> **Disclaimer:** This is offline emergency first aid guidance. It is NOT a substitute for professional medical training or emergency services. Always call emergency services (112 or local number) for life-threatening situations.

## Burns

### Minor Burns (First Degree — redness, no blisters)
1. Cool immediately: Hold under cool (not cold or icy) running water for 10 to 20 minutes.
2. Do NOT apply butter, toothpaste, oil, or ice — these cause more damage.
3. Cover loosely with a clean non-fluffy bandage or cling film.
4. Take paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain.
5. See a doctor if the burn is on the face, hands, genitals, or larger than 3cm.

### Serious Burns (Second/Third Degree — blisters or charred skin)
1. Call emergency services immediately.
2. Do NOT remove clothing stuck to the burn.
3. Do NOT burst blisters.
4. Cover loosely with a clean dressing or cling film.
5. Keep the person warm to prevent shock.

## Choking

### Conscious Adult or Child (over 1 year)
1. Ask: "Are you choking?" If they cannot speak, cough, or breathe — act immediately.
2. Give up to 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. If back blows fail, give up to 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich Manoeuvre):
   - Stand behind. Place one fist above the navel, thumb inward.
   - Cover with other hand. Pull sharply inward and upward.
4. Alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until object is dislodged.
5. Call emergency services if unconscious.

### Infant (under 1 year)
1. Give 5 gentle back blows face-down on your forearm.
2. Give 5 chest thrusts (NOT abdominal thrusts) with 2 fingers on the breastbone.
3. Check mouth between each cycle. Call emergency services if not resolved quickly.

## Bleeding

### Minor Bleeding
1. Clean hands. Apply firm pressure with a clean cloth or bandage.
2. Maintain pressure for at least 10 minutes.
3. Elevate the injured area above heart level.
4. Once bleeding stops, clean wound and apply antiseptic.

### Severe Bleeding
1. Call emergency services immediately.
2. Apply very firm, direct pressure with both hands.
3. Do NOT remove the object if an object is embedded — press around it.
4. If a limb is bleeding severely, apply a tourniquet above the wound if trained.
5. Keep applying pressure until help arrives. Do NOT remove the cloth — add more on top.

## CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

### Adult
1. Check if person is responsive — tap shoulders, shout.
2. Call emergency services or have someone call.
3. 30 chest compressions: Place heel of hand on centre of chest, press down 5 to 6cm at rate of 100 to 120 per minute.
4. 2 rescue breaths: Tilt head back, lift chin, pinch nose, give breath for 1 second each.
5. Repeat 30:2 ratio until emergency services arrive or AED is available.

### Child (1 to 12 years)
- Same 30:2 ratio. Use one hand for compressions. Press down one third of chest depth.

### Infant (under 1 year)
- Use 2 fingers on breastbone. 30 compressions to 2 gentle puffs.

## Fractures & Sprains

### Suspected Fracture
1. Do NOT attempt to straighten the limb.
2. Immobilise by supporting in the position found.
3. Apply ice pack wrapped in cloth to reduce swelling. Do not apply ice directly.
4. Seek immediate medical attention.

### Sprain
1. RICE: Rest, Ice (20 mins on, 20 mins off), Compression bandage, Elevate.
2. Take ibuprofen for swelling and pain.
3. See a doctor if unable to bear weight or severe swelling.

## Fainting
1. Help person lie down safely. Raise legs above heart level.
2. Loosen tight clothing around neck and waist.
3. Ensure fresh air.
4. Do NOT give water or food until fully conscious.
5. Seek medical help if person does not regain consciousness within 1 minute.

## Anaphylaxis (Severe Allergic Reaction)
Signs: hives, swelling of lips/throat, difficulty breathing, rapid weak pulse, dizziness.
1. Call emergency services immediately — this is life-threatening.
2. Use EpiPen (adrenaline auto-injector) if available — inject into outer thigh.
3. Lay person flat with legs raised unless breathing is difficult (sit them up).
4. Give second EpiPen after 5 minutes if no improvement.
5. Begin CPR if they lose consciousness and stop breathing.
